## Song Info

- **Title:** The Christmas Song (Chestnuts Roasting on an Open Fire)
- **Composer:** Mel Tormé / Robert Wells (1945)
- **Center Key:** C Major (also recorded in D♭, B♭)
- **Form:** 32 bars AABA (each section 8 bars)
- **Source:** The Jazz Pursuit — "The Christmas Song (Wells/Tormé) Analysis & Tutorial" (https://youtu.be/2wOCu_mqK1Y)

### Diatonic Chords (C Major)

CMaj7 (I), Dm7 (ii), Em7 (iii), FMaj7 (IV), G7 (V), Am7 (vi), Bm7♭5 (vii°)

### Chord Progression

**A section (8 bars):**
| CMaj7 Dm7 | Em7 FMaj7 | CMaj7 | Gm7 C7 FMaj7 | Am7 | CMaj7 | F#m7 B7 (EMaj7) | Fm7 Bb7 (EbMaj7) → Dm7 G7 |

**B section (8 bars):**
| Gm7 C7 | FMaj7 | Gm7 C7 | FMaj7 | Fm7 Bb7 | EbMaj7 | Am7 D7 | Dm7 G7 |

## Level 1: General Concepts

### 1-1. Walking through the chords of the key

**instruction:** How does the A section begin?

**response:** It walks straight up the diatonic chords of C major: CMaj7 (I) → Dm7 (ii) → Em7 (iii) → FMaj7 (IV), then falls back to CMaj7 (I). This stepwise climb through the key's own chords is a simple way to establish the home key clearly before the harmony starts to wander. It also sets up the three landing points the A section visits — I, IV, and the relative minor vi.

### 1-2. ii-V-I cadences to non-tonic targets

**instruction:** How does the A section reach chord IV and the relative minor?

**response:** With short ii-V-I cadences aimed at each target. To tonicize IV (FMaj7), it uses Gm7 – C7 – FMaj7 (a ii-V-I treating F as a temporary "one"). Then it touches chord vi, the relative minor Am7, before returning to CMaj7. Treating a non-tonic chord as a momentary key center and approaching it with its own ii-V is the engine that drives most jazz harmony.

### 1-3. Modulating ii-Vs at the end of the A section

**instruction:** Why does the A section leave the key near the end?

**response:** The end of the first A runs two ii-V-Is to surprising major targets: one to E major (F♯m7 – B7 – EMaj7, tonicizing chord iii as a major chord) and one to E♭ major (Fm7 – B♭7 – EbMaj7, tonicizing ♭III). These bright, out-of-key arrivals are the song's harmonic signature. A final ii-V (Dm7 – G7) then pulls back to CMaj7 for the next A.

## Level 2: Song-Specific Analysis

### 2-1. The A section in full

**instruction:** Analyze the Christmas Song A section.

**response:** Bars 1–2 walk up the key (CMaj7 – Dm7 – Em7 – FMaj7), then fall to CMaj7. A ii-V-I to IV (Gm7 – C7 – FMaj7) follows, then a move to chord vi (Am7) and back to CMaj7 — hitting I, IV, and vi, the key's main pillars. The end leaves home with two ii-V-Is, to EMaj7 (chord iii major) and EbMaj7 (♭III), before a ii-V (Dm7 – G7) resolves back to C for the repeat.

### 2-2. The second-time ending and the bridge

**instruction:** How do the second A ending and the bridge work?

**response:** In the second ending, the bar-7 cadence that went to EMaj7 instead resolves to chord iii minor (Em7), launching a 3-6-2-5-1 (Em7 – Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – CMaj7); making the vi chord dominant (A7) adds momentum. The bridge then runs a ii-V-I to IV (Gm7 – C7 – FMaj7) twice, turns FMaj7 minor (Fm7) for a ii-V-I to EbMaj7, then moves a tritone away to A and uses Am7 – D7, D7 turning minor (Dm7) – G7, back to CMaj7 for the final A.

## Level 3: Performance Decisions

### 3-1. Spelling the out-of-key arrivals

**instruction:** What do I play over the ii-Vs to EMaj7 and EbMaj7?

**response:** Treat each as its own key for a moment. Over F♯m7 – B7 lean toward E major (approach the 3rd of E, G♯, from a half-step above as the melody does); over Fm7 – B♭7 think E♭ major and walk down the E♭ scale toward an E♭ chord tone. The melody resolves these by stepping cleanly onto a chord tone of each new tonic, so target notes — not busy scales — make the bright modulations land.

### 3-2. Common 5-1 and backdoor reharms in the A section

**instruction:** Where can I add cadences to richen the A section?

**response:** Three spots take a 5-1: before the CMaj7 return (G7 → CMaj7), before the Am7 (E7 → Am7), and before the final CMaj7 (G7 → CMaj7). For more color, swap that last 5-1 for a backdoor cadence — B♭7 resolving up a whole step to CMaj7 (B♭7 is ♭VII7) — for a softer, hipper arrival than a plain G7.

### 3-3. The ascending-bass reharmonization

**instruction:** How can I get a rising bass line through the opening?

**response:** Keep the chords of the key but pedal the bass upward: CMaj7, then CMaj7/G (chord I over its 5th), an E7 voiced over its 3rd (G♯) as a 5-1 into Am7, then a backdoor B♭7 up to CMaj7. This delays the root-position tonic and gives a smooth, stepwise bass climb. It's harmonically dense, so use it as a special-occasion reharm rather than on every A section.
