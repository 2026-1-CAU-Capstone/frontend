## Song Info

- **Title:** Moon River
- **Composer:** Henry Mancini / Johnny Mercer (1961, from *Breakfast at Tiffany's*)
- **Center Key:** C Major (also recorded in F, G)
- **Form:** 40 bars, A B A C (A and B are 8 bars, C is 16 bars); 3/4 ballad
- **Source:** The Jazz Pursuit — "Moon River (H. Mancini) Analysis & Tutorial" (https://youtu.be/Gt8zN6UuP1I)

### Diatonic Chords (C Major)

CMaj7 (I), Dm7 (ii), Em7 (iii), FMaj7 (IV), G7 (V), Am7 (vi), Bm7♭5 (vii°)

### Chord Progression

**A section (8 bars):**
| CMaj7 | Am7 | FMaj7 | CMaj7 | FMaj7 | CMaj7 | Bm7♭5 E7 | Am7 |

**B section (8 bars, foundation):**
| Am7 | Am7 | FMaj7 | FMaj7 | Am7 | Am7 | Dm7 | G7 |

**C section (16 bars):**
| Am7 Am7/G | F#m7♭5 FMaj7 | CMaj7 | FMaj7 | CMaj7 | FMaj7 | CMaj7 Am7 | Dm7 G7 CMaj7 |

## Level 1: General Concepts

### 1-1. Outlining the key's main landing points

**instruction:** How does the A section establish C major?

**response:** It tours the three pillars of the key: chord I (CMaj7), chord vi / relative minor (Am7), and chord IV (FMaj7), then back to I. It even repeats the IV-I move (FMaj7 → CMaj7). By visiting I, IV, and vi up front, the A section plants the home key firmly before any cadential motion. These are the most common "landing points" in any major key.

### 1-2. The minor ii-V-i to the relative minor

**instruction:** How does the A section end?

**response:** With a minor ii-V-i aimed at chord vi (Am7). Because the target is a minor chord, the cadence borrows from A harmonic minor: the ii is half-diminished (Bm7♭5), the V is dominant (E7), resolving to Am7. So Bm7♭5 – E7 – Am7 tonicizes the relative minor. In every minor ii-V-i the pattern is the same — half-diminished ii, dominant V, minor i.

### 1-3. The "stationary chord, moving bass" cliché

**instruction:** What is the descending-bass figure that opens the C section?

**response:** It's a line where the chord stays put while the bass descends chromatically — the "On Green Dolphin Street" device. Over a held A-minor sonority the bass walks A → G → F♯ → F: Am → Am7/G → Am7/F♯ → Am7/F. Reading the slash chords by their bass notes, Am7/F♯ is actually F♯m7♭5 (half-diminished) and Am7/F is FMaj7. So one smooth chromatic bass descent quietly spells three different chords.

## Level 2: Song-Specific Analysis

### 2-1. The A and B sections

**instruction:** Compare the A and B sections harmonically.

**response:** The A section changes chord every bar (I – vi – IV – I – IV – I – minor ii-V – vi), a fairly quick harmonic rhythm. The B section slows down, holding each chord two bars: it starts on Am7 (where the A left off), moves to FMaj7, back to Am7, then a ii-V-I (Dm7 – G7 – CMaj7) returns home for the repeat of A. So B is a relaxed, slow-moving contrast to A's busier motion, then A returns before the long C section.

### 2-2. The C section

**instruction:** Walk me through the 16-bar C section.

**response:** It opens like the B section on Am7, then runs the descending-bass cliché (Am → Am7/G → F♯m7♭5 → FMaj7), resolving to CMaj7. It restates the I-IV motion twice (CMaj7 → FMaj7), then closes the whole form with a 1-6-2-5-1 (CMaj7 – Am7 – Dm7 – G7 – CMaj7). The melody peaks here on the high D (the 2nd) — the highest note of the tune — set against a falling bass, so melody and bass move in contrary motion and the music feels like it's opening up.

## Level 3: Performance Decisions

### 3-1. The Lydian color over chord IV

**instruction:** What scale fits the FMaj7 chords in the melody?

**response:** The melody plays a B♮ over FMaj7 — the ♯11 (♯4) — which turns the chord's scale from F major into F Lydian. So over the IV chord, think F Lydian rather than plain F major, and treat the ♯11 (B) as a target color note the way the original melody does, walking down the Lydian scale to the root F. That bright ♯11 is one of Moon River's signature melodic sounds.

### 3-2. Adding cadences to the B section

**instruction:** How can I keep the slow B section moving?

**response:** Use the second bar of each two-bar chord to set up the next with a 5-1. Before the FMaj7 in bar 3, insert C7 (V of F); before the Am7 in bar 5, insert E7 (V of Am). So Am7 → C7 → FMaj7, then FMaj7 → E7 → Am7. These secondary dominants animate the otherwise static two-bar chords without changing the underlying harmony.

### 3-3. A chain of ii-Vs into the final cadence

**instruction:** How can I reharmonize the B section's closing ii-V-I for more drive?

**response:** Replace the plain Dm7 – G7 – CMaj7 with a string of ii-Vs starting a tritone away from the tonic: F♯m7♭5 – B7, then E7 becomes Em7 to start the next ii-V (Em7 – A7), then A7's target D becomes Dm7 (Dm7 – G7 – CMaj7). So F♯m7♭5 – B7 – Em7 – A7 – Dm7 – G7 – CMaj7 — a descending cycle of overlapping ii-Vs that gives the resolution far more momentum than a single ii-V.
