## Song Info

- **Title:** Misty
- **Composer:** Erroll Garner (1954)
- **Center Key:** E♭ Major
- **Form:** 32 bars AABA (each section 8 bars)
- **Source:** The Jazz Pursuit — "Misty (E. Garner) Tutorial & Analysis" (https://youtu.be/cCoNNQAuyjg)

### Diatonic Chords (E♭ Major)

EbMaj7 (I), Fm7 (ii), Gm7 (iii), AbMaj7 (IV), Bb7 (V), Cm7 (vi), Dm7♭5 (vii°)

### Chord Progression

**A section (8 bars):**
| EbMaj7 | Bbm7 Eb7 | AbMaj7 | Abm7 Db7 | EbMaj7 Cm7 | Fm7 Bb7 | EbMaj7 (Cm7) | Fm7 Bb7 |

## Level 1: General Concepts

### 1-1. The ii-V-I to chord IV

**instruction:** How does Misty move from chord I to chord IV so smoothly?

**response:** Misty starts on chord I (EbMaj7), then uses a ii-V-I aimed at chord IV. To tonicize A♭ (chord IV), it treats A♭ as a temporary "one," so the ii-V-I in A♭ is Bbm7 – Eb7 – AbMaj7. Notice E♭7 here is a dominant 7th (not the EbMaj7 tonic) because it is functioning as the V of A♭. This "ii-V into IV" is a classic way to give the subdominant its own moment of arrival before returning home.

### 1-2. The backdoor: IV turning minor (Abm7 – Db7 → I)

**instruction:** Why does A♭ major turn into A♭ minor right after?

**response:** After arriving on AbMaj7 (IV), Misty turns it minor — Abm7 — and pairs it with Db7 to form a backdoor cadence home. Abm7 – Db7 → EbMaj7 resolves up a whole step (D♭7 is ♭VII7 of E♭), borrowing the darker minor-subdominant color before landing back on the tonic. The shift from bright AbMaj7 to wistful Abm7 is one of the most recognizable sounds in the tune.

### 1-3. The 1-6-2-5-1 turnaround

**instruction:** What is a "1-6-2-5-1" and where does Misty use it?

**response:** A 1-6-2-5-1 is one of the most common harmonic chunks in jazz: I → vi → ii → V → I. In E♭ that is EbMaj7 → Cm7 → Fm7 → Bb7 → EbMaj7. Misty uses it to close the A section, circling back to the tonic through the cycle of fifths. The vi chord (Cm7) can also be substituted with a dominant (C7) to add extra pull, but the diatonic vi keeps the smooth ballad color.

## Level 2: Song-Specific Analysis

### 2-1. The A section in full

**instruction:** Analyze the Misty A section.

**response:** It opens on chord I (EbMaj7). A ii-V-I aimed at IV follows: Bbm7 – Eb7 – AbMaj7, giving the subdominant its own arrival. AbMaj7 then turns minor (Abm7) and, with Db7, forms a backdoor cadence resolving up a step to EbMaj7. The section closes with a 1-6-2-5-1 turnaround (EbMaj7 – Cm7 – Fm7 – Bb7 – EbMaj7). So the A section is a tour of the home key's main pillars — I, the ii-V into IV, the backdoor return, and the cycle-of-fifths turnaround.

### 2-2. Why E♭7 is dominant, not major

**instruction:** Isn't E♭ the tonic? Why is it written E♭7 in bar 2?

**response:** The tonic chord is EbMaj7, but in bar 2 the E♭ chord is a dominant 7th (Eb7) because it is no longer functioning as "one" — it is the V of A♭. In the ii-V-I to IV (Bbm7 – Eb7 – AbMaj7), E♭7 is a secondary dominant pulling to A♭. Same root, different function: as a tonic it's major 7th, as a secondary dominant it's a dominant 7th. Reading the function tells you which scale to use.

## Level 3: Performance Decisions

### 3-1. Scale choices through the A section

**instruction:** What scales fit the Misty A section?

**response:** Over EbMaj7 (I) use E♭ major (or Lydian). Over the ii-V into IV (Bbm7 – Eb7 – AbMaj7) think A♭ major, with E♭7 as a Mixolydian dominant resolving to A♭. Over the backdoor Abm7 – Db7 use A♭ Dorian / D♭ Mixolydian (D♭ Lydian-dominant for the ♯11 color) resolving back to E♭. Over the closing 1-6-2-5-1 stay in E♭ major, optionally adding ♭9/♭13 on Bb7 for tension into the tonic.

### 3-2. Voicing the AbMaj7 → Abm7 color

**instruction:** How do I bring out the major-to-minor IV move when comping or soloing?

**response:** The signature is the C♮ in AbMaj7 dropping to C♭ (B♮) in Abm7. Highlight that descending half-step in your line or top voice — it's the emotional hinge of the phrase. Soloing, target the major 3rd of A♭ (C) on AbMaj7, then resolve it down to the minor 3rd (C♭/B) as the chord turns to Abm7, before the Db7 carries you home to E♭. That single voice-leading move captures the tune's bittersweet character.

### 3-3. Ballad approach over a cycle-of-fifths turnaround

**instruction:** How should I phrase over the 1-6-2-5-1 ending each A section?

**response:** Because EbMaj7 – Cm7 – Fm7 – Bb7 – EbMaj7 is a cycle-of-fifths loop entirely in E♭ major, you don't need to change scales — you can play E♭ major across all of it and let guide-tone voice leading do the work. As a ballad, use the turnaround as a place to land and breathe, resolving onto a clear E♭ chord tone. If you want more motion, treat Cm7 as C7 (vi→VI) to add a secondary-dominant lift toward Fm7.
